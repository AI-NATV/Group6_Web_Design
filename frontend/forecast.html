1. Define your forecasting task

How to configure a forecast

Time column
Choose the column that orders observations in time, usually Date. After duplicate dates are handled, each time point needs one value.
Target to predict
Choose the numeric value to forecast, such as Close/Last or Revenue. Do not choose information that will be unknown at forecast time.
Horizon
The number of future periods to estimate. Start with 7. For stocks with market holidays, this means 7 observations, not necessarily 7 calendar days.
Frequency
Keep Detect from dates unless you know the data are complete days, weeks or months. Use Observation steps for irregular schedules.
Invalid, missing and duplicate rows
Stop and report is the safe option. Use Skip, Sum or Mean only when that rule reflects the meaning of your data.
Season length
Leave 0 when you do not know that the series repeats. For example, use 12 for monthly data with annual seasonality.

One series per run. The only input to each model is past target values; other columns are not used as predictors.

Forecast settings

Season length is measured in observations (e.g. 12 for monthly annual seasonality). Only enable it when the repeating cycle makes sense. Dates must be normalized as YYYY-MM-DD; targets must be numeric.

2. Check performance on past data

Two expanding training windows predict the next full horizon. Lowest validation MAE selects the model. The final test window is kept separate from selection.

MAE is the average absolute error in target units. RMSE penalizes larger errors more. Lower is better; neither is a probability or confidence score.
